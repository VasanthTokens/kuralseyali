Who is your audience?

Primary users: small independent service tradespeople — electricians, plumbers, AC/appliance repair techs, and similar one-person or small-crew businesses, especially in Tier 2/3 Indian cities and Tamil-speaking regions. Think of people like Ramesh: skilled at their trade, always on the move between job sites, but with no formal office, no receptionist, no CRM software — just a phone and WhatsApp.

Secondary beneficiary: their customers — regular people who don't want to type out a request; it's much easier to just send a voice note saying "fan not working, come tomorrow."

Why is it genuinely helpful for them?

This solves a few real, everyday pain points:

No more lost or forgotten jobs. Right now, most solo tradespeople manage requests by memory, or by scrolling back through WhatsApp chats — easy to lose track when you get 10+ voice notes a day from different customers.
No typing required, on either end. The customer speaks naturally in Tamil/Hinglish, and Ramesh doesn't have to manually write anything down — the system does the structuring.
A single place to see everything. Instead of mentally juggling "did I finish the Anna Nagar job? What about MG Road?", the dashboard gives a real-time, organized view — pending vs in-progress vs done.
Removes a literacy/tech barrier. Many tradespeople aren't comfortable with typing-heavy apps or English-only software. Voice-first input + native language output (your Tamil job card idea) makes this accessible to people who'd never adopt a typical SaaS CRM.
Zero cost, zero training needed. They already use WhatsApp daily — this doesn't ask them to learn a new app, just to forward voice notes to one number.
Is it a good hackathon project solving a real problem?

Yes, genuinely — and here's why it stands out:

It's not a toy problem. Job/task tracking for informal-sector workers is an underserved area — most software targets big businesses, not a one-person electrician.
The tech choices are smart for the constraint (no budget): WhatsApp (channel people already use) + free-tier AI (voice-to-structured-data) + free Sheets (storage) + free dashboard hosting — this whole thing costs nothing to run at small scale.
You've made it multilingual, which is a real accessibility win judges tend to notice — most hackathon projects stay English-only and ignore that huge chunk of India's workforce doesn't primarily operate in English.
It's demoable end-to-end in under a minute — voice note in, job card out, live dashboard update. That's a strong, clear demo arc for judges.
